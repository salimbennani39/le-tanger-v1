Private collection — Khalid Bennani

A history of the telephone.

From the crank to the dial, a collection of antique telephones gathered over the years and displayed in the reception lounge.

In the reception lounge

A private collection of telephones.

On the wooden shelves at reception, each set tells a chapter in the history of the telephone: the age of operators, the arrival of the dial, then the models made for the living room. The collection is open to everyone, residents and visitors alike: come and see it, and ask us to tell you its story.

01 · Late 19th – early 20th century

The hand-cranked wall telephone.

The oldest in the collection. A wooden box hung on the wall, two metal bells, a horn to speak into and a receiver you lift to listen.

No dial yet. To make a call, you turned the crank: it drove a small generator, the “magneto”, which rang the exchange. An operator, one of the “telephone girls”, answered, asked who you wished to speak to and connected the call by hand, plug by plug.

02 · 1950s

The rotary desk telephone.

With the rotary dial, callers dial the number themselves: the automatic exchange gradually replaces the operator. In Paris, the first automatic exchange opened in 1928, and it was not until 1979 that the whole French network worked without operators.

The keys lined up at the front were used to switch between lines or put a call on hold. A heavy, sturdy office set, built to last for decades.

03 · 1960s – 1970s

The onyx and brass telephone.

A nod to the Belle Époque: the handset rests on a brass cradle, as on sets from the turn of the century, but a modern dial sits on its veined onyx front.

These showpieces were made to be seen. They took pride of place on a console or a desk, as much decoration as telephone.

Key dates

From the switchboard to the dial.

  1. 1876Alexander Graham Bell patents the telephone.
  2. 1879The first telephone exchange opens in Paris. Calls go through operators.
  3. 1928First automatic exchange in Paris: callers dial the number themselves.
  4. 1979The French network becomes fully automatic. The age of operators comes to an end.
